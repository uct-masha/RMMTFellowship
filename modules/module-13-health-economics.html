13. Health Economics and Financing

Overview

This module introduces participants to the role of health economics and financing in supporting malaria policy and programme decisions. Participants explore how modelling and economic evidence can inform prioritisation, resource allocation, and investment decisions in resource-constrained settings.

TipLearning Objectives

By the end of this module, participants should be able to:

  • Explain how economic evidence can support policy and programme decisions

  • Identify what information is needed to assess affordability, sustainability, and value for money

  • Understand how modelling can inform funding allocations and investment strategies

NoteKey Concepts

  • Cost-effectiveness and value for money

  • Resource allocation and prioritisation

  • Affordability and sustainability

  • Investment cases and advocacy

  • Economic interpretation of modelling outputs

Practical Exercise

Exercise Title: Health economics for decision-making

Instructions:

For the modelling question(s) in your Capstone Project, discuss the following:

  • What is the budget context of your setting (total budget, annual budget, intervention budget)?

  • What economic analyses would you want to be conducted? What outcomes would be of interest?

  • Map the costs associated with these analyses. 

  • Go through the CHEERS checklist, completing items in the methods sections

  • Does your country have a WTP threshold? How would this influence decision-making?

Note on timing: This exercise can be incorporated into the main Health Economics session and continued in the Capstone Project group work session, if more time is needed.

Facilitator Guidance

  • Focus on practical interpretation rather than technical economic methods

  • Reinforce that economic evidence supports—not replaces—policy judgement

  • Encourage discussion on operational realities and funding constraints

  • Use relatable programme examples and investment cases

CautionPractical reflections from delivering the inaugural cohort

We allocated a 3-hour session to this module, which allowed us to cover the core concepts. However, this topic was new to most fellows and was of particular interest—feedback indicated that there was appetite for more health economic content. In future iterations, we recommend extending the session or adding an additional follow-up session to allow for deeper engagement with the material and more time for group exercises and discussion.
